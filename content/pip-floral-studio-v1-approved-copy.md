## Flowers that shape the day
# Setting the scene with flowers.
Flowers can change the atmosphere of a wedding. They bring colour, movement and life into a venue, connect different parts of the day and make a space feel ready for the people arriving in it.
They can be quiet and simple or completely transform a room. The right approach depends on the venue, the season and how you want the wedding to feel.
I’ll help you decide where flowers will make the most difference, then design each element as part of the same overall idea.
## Wedding highlights
# A few possibilities.
There are many ways flowers can become part of a wedding. You can focus on a small number of personal pieces, carry a design through the whole day or build everything around one memorable installation.

## Designed for your wedding
# Finding the right floral direction.
Our first conversations are about the wedding as a whole. I want to understand you, the atmosphere you want, the colours you are drawn to and the parts of the day that feel most important.
If you already have a clear idea, I’ll help develop it and work out how it can function within the space. If you are unsure, I’ll guide you through the options and help you find a direction.
The season also shapes the design. I choose flowers for their colour, form, texture and character, using locally grown and British flowers as much as is practical.
The result should feel connected to the wedding around it and natural within the venue.
## What’s possible
# Flowers throughout your wedding.
### Personal flowers
Bouquets, buttonholes, posies and flower crowns are made for the people wearing or carrying them. Colour, shape, clothing and comfort can all influence the design.
### Ceremony flowers
Aisle meadows, urns, arches, entrance flowers and arrangements for the ceremony table can frame the setting and draw attention to the ceremony itself.
### Reception flowers
Table flowers can range from groups of bud vases to larger bowl arrangements. Flowers can also be used around bars, cakes, fireplaces, staircases and other parts of the venue.
### Installations
Hanging flowers, flower clouds, large arches and other installations can change the balance and atmosphere of a space. Their design depends on the venue, access, structure and time available for installation.
### Moving flowers between spaces
Where the arrangements allow, ceremony flowers can be moved and used again at the reception. I’ll plan this in advance and manage the changeover with the venue.
You do not need to decide which of these you want before enquiring. They are starting points for our conversation.

## Before you enquire
# Start with what you know.
Most couples enquire once they have a date and venue, but you do not need to have either confirmed.
You might already have a detailed moodboard. You might only know how you want the wedding to feel. Tell me what you have planned so far and we can begin from there.
My diary is normally open for the current and following calendar year. If you are planning further ahead, you are still welcome to enquire.
Six months gives us a comfortable amount of time to plan and prepare, although I can often work within a shorter timeframe.
[Enquire about your wedding]
## Your enquiry
# Tell me about the wedding.
The enquiry form asks for the information I need to understand your plans and decide how best to help.
I’ll ask about your date, venue or likely location, the type of flowers you are considering and the ideas you have so far. You can also share a link to a moodboard if you have one.
I aim to reply within three working days. If I’m available, I’ll invite you to arrange a free discovery call.
## The discovery call
# A chance to explore the ideas.
The discovery call usually lasts between 20 and 40 minutes.
We’ll talk about the venue, what you have booked, the flowers or installations you are considering and any budget you have in mind. I’ll help you work through the options if you are unsure where to begin.
The call also gives you a chance to understand how I work and how involved you would like to be during the planning.
Calls can take place by video or telephone. We can also meet in person where practical.
After I have accepted your enquiry, you’ll receive a private link to book a suitable time.
## Your initial design
# Bringing the ideas together.
After our call, I’ll create a moodboard showing the initial floral direction. This will bring together colour, shape, texture and examples of how the designs could work within your venue.
You’ll also receive an indicative, itemised quotation covering the elements we discussed.
The first quotation often shows the fuller range of possibilities. It gives us something useful to review together. I’ll help you decide which elements matter most and where the budget will have the greatest effect.
The moodboard and initial quotation are provided without charge and are bespoke to you.
I’ll confirm when you can expect to receive them after our call.
## Reviewing the proposal
# Time to consider the options.
Once you have had time to look through the moodboard and quotation, we can arrange a follow-up call or chat via email.
We’ll discuss what you love, anything you would like to change and how the different elements work within the budget. I’ll then update the design and quotation accordingly.
## Booking Pip
# Securing your date.
When you are happy to proceed, payment of the design fee secures your wedding date. This is 20% of the estimated total.
The design fee is deducted from your final wedding balance. Before it is paid, I may hold the date informally while we are actively discussing the proposal.
Once booked, we can continue developing the flowers as the wider wedding plans take shape.
## Planning together
# As much guidance as you need.
Some couples want to discuss every detail. Others prefer to agree the overall direction and leave more of the decisions with me.
We can communicate by video, telephone, email or in person. I provide written follow-ups so that decisions and next steps are clear.
A venue visit can be arranged when the design or logistics require one. In-person meetings may also take place at the venue or another suitable location.
I can adapt the pace and format of meetings to you. There’s no fixed timeline, so whether you’d like everything sorted months in advance or you need a bit longer to decide quite how many of your extended family need a buttonhole, we can work together to ensure that a final plan is in place one month before the wedding.
## Final details
# Confirming the plans.
A couple of months before the wedding, I’ll begin gathering any details that are still outstanding.
This may include final guest numbers, buttonhole quantities, confirmed arrangements, access times and practical information from the venue.
I’ll issue the final invoice once the plans have been agreed, with final payment due four weeks before the wedding day. Except in unusual circumstances, payment confirms the final design and quantities.
## Ordering and preparation
# The flowers arrive.
Around two weeks before the wedding, I finalise the flower orders and liaise with growers and suppliers.
During the wedding week, the flowers arrive at the studio. They are conditioned and prepared before I begin making the bouquets and arrangements.
Installation elements are built at the studio where possible. This reduces the work needed at the venue and helps the setup run smoothly.
The exact flowers may depend on the season and what is looking its best. The agreed colour, shape and overall design guide the final selection.
## At the venue
# Setting everything in place.
Large installations may begin the day before the wedding, with final flowers added on the day.
I’ll deliver bouquets, buttonholes and other personal flowers to wherever you are getting ready. I’ll then install the ceremony flowers and prepare the reception spaces.
If flowers are being moved from the ceremony to the reception, I’ll manage the changeover with the venue team.
I coordinate access and timings directly with the venue and other suppliers. You will usually only see me when I deliver the personal flowers. The rest is handled behind the scenes, leaving you to enjoy the day!
## After the wedding
# Making the most of the flowers.
I’ll return to the venue the following day to clear the arrangements and installation materials.
Flowers that are still looking good can be gathered into bunches for you or your guests to take home. Flowers and foliage that are looking too tired from the day before are returned to the studio and composted.

## A life spent making
# Flowers have always been part of my life.
I grew up in a family of gardeners. Flowers, foliage and plants were part of everyday life, along with the care and patience involved in helping things grow.
That environment gave me a love of nature, colour and seasonal change. It still influences the flowers I choose and the way I put them together.
I later studied textile design and worked for several years as a self-employed weaver and designer. The materials were different, but I was still working with colour, form, texture and space, turning an idea into something physical.
When I was ready for a new creative challenge, floristry felt like a natural direction. I completed formal training and established Pip Floral Studio in 2021.
## A maker at heart
# I’m happiest when I’m creating.
I have always needed to make things. The medium may change, but the process of exploring an idea, working through its possibilities and bringing it into the world remains the same.
Floristry gives me the freedom to work at different scales. It might be a single buttonhole, a bouquet full of movement or an installation that changes the feeling of a room.
Every project presents a new combination of people, place, colour and season. That is what keeps the work interesting.
